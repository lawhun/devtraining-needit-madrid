## 1. Why Grant is the right target

Bryce's instinct is correct — this is one of the premier paramedic programs in central Ohio:

- **Full name:** Franklin County Firefighters (FCFF) | Grant Medical Center EMS Education — marketed as **OhioHealth EMS Education**.
- **Heritage:** Founded **December 1983** as a partnership between OhioHealth Grant Medical Center's **LifeFlight** program and the **Franklin County Firefighter's Association**. Deep fire-service roots — this program was *built by and for firefighters*.
- **Track record:** **40+ years**, **2,000+ graduates**, and a **~95% National Registry (NREMT) pass rate** — well above typical.
- **Accreditation:** Approved by the **Ohio Department of Public Safety, Division of EMS**, and nationally accredited by **CAAHEP** (Commission on Accreditation of Allied Health Education Programs). National accreditation is what lets graduates sit for the **NREMT** exam — non-negotiable for portability and for most hiring departments.

For a firefighter/EMT looking to become a firefighter-paramedic, this is about as strong a pedigree as central Ohio offers.

## 4. Program format, length & cost

- **Length:** ~**14 months** of instruction (students are engaged with the school for roughly **15 months** start to finish).
- **Hours:** **500+ hours** classroom & patient simulation **plus 500+ hours** of clinical/field experience.
- **Schedule:** Designed around fire-service life — classes meet on **weekday "2-Unit duty dates" with some Saturday/Sunday sessions**. Day vs. night class options exist. This is deliberately built so working firefighters can attend around shift rotations.
- **Cohorts:** The school runs **multiple staggered classes at once** (often 4–5 cohorts in progress), each with its own start and graduation date.
- **Tuition:** **$5,600 and up** (varies by day vs. night class and by the EMS department's affiliation with the program). Tuition **includes**: textbooks, labs, standardized specialty courses (**BLS, ACLS, AMLS, PALS, PEARS, PEPP, PHTLS**), **initial National Registry testing**, uniform shirt, and priority on-campus parking. **Payment plans may be available.** Ask about the **department-sponsored / affiliated rate** — it can be lower.

## 5. Closing the A&P gap (the likely blocker)

If Grant confirms Bryce still needs Anatomy & Physiology, knock it out fast so it doesn't cost him a cohort:

- **Columbus State Community College** — offers A&P (in-person and online); the natural local option and commonly accepted.
- **Other regional community colleges** (e.g., Central Ohio Technical College) and **accredited online A&P courses** — confirm acceptance with Grant *before* enrolling in any course, so it counts.
- Ask Grant specifically: *"Does a single-semester Human A&P satisfy the requirement, or do you require A&P I and II? Do you accept online courses?"*

Getting Bryce **accepted and enrolled** is the lever that opens the paramedic-track roles the scout keeps flagging as ineligible:

- **Now → enrolled:** Many departments hire **firefighter/EMT recruits with a medic-school commitment** and several post **"paramedic student" or "medic-in-training"** roles.
